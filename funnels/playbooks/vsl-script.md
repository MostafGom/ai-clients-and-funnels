# VSL script structure (12–14 minutes, 1,800–2,200 words at ~150 wpm)

Format for `VSL-SCRIPT.md`:
```
## [0:00] Chapter title
> art: stars        (which illustration the player shows)
> reveal            (optional: the CTA/offer unlocks when this chapter starts)
One paragraph per slide. Short. Spoken rhythm.
```

| Time | Chapter | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Hook | Pattern interrupt + the promise + who it's for + "stay to the end for X" | 40–60 s |
| 0:45 | The night | Founder's own story in the problem. Specific, sensory, one scene. | 90 s |
| 2:30 | The lie | The common belief that keeps them stuck, and why it's wrong | 90 s |
| 4:00 | The mechanism | Name it. Teach it with one visual. Make them say "oh, that's why." | 3 min |
| 7:00 | Proof | 2–3 fast case studies with numbers + the founder's credentials | 2 min |
| 9:00 | The offer (reveal) | What it is, who it's for, what's inside (stack), what it costs, the anchor | 2.5 min |
| 11:30 | Guarantee + objections | Guarantee in full; 3 fastest objections answered | 90 s |
| 13:00 | Close | Future pace 7 nights out; exact instruction (click, what happens next) | 60 s |

Rules
- The offer is never mentioned before the reveal chapter. The page hides the offer until then.
- Each slide ≤ 25 words. Spoken English. Contractions. Second person.
- One visual per chapter, not per slide. The visual teaches; it doesn't decorate.
- The scripted stand-in plays at reading pace: 2.2 s + 0.12 s per word per slide, clamped 3–5 s (about half the spoken length). The recorded video runs at speaking pace; its timestamps become `revealAfter`.
