## p2 · The cost of waiting
The same $500 a month, started at 35 vs at 45, at an illustrative 7% average yearly return, compounded monthly, with no fees or taxes:
- start at 35 → about $610k at 65 (you put in $180k)
- start at 45 → about $260k at 65 (you put in $120k)
That's not a forecast. Real returns vary, some years are negative, and you can lose money. The point is the shape: time does more of the work than the amount.
**Before you invest anything:** keep an emergency fund of 3–6 months of expenses in savings, and pay off high-interest debt (credit cards) first.

## p3 · The order
1. **401(k) up to the match.** If your employer matches, contribute at least enough to get all of it. It's part of your pay.
2. **Roth IRA.** Contributions go in after tax, and qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. There are income limits and a yearly maximum.
3. **Back to the 401(k)**, above the match, if you have room.
4. **Brokerage account**, for anything beyond that, or for goals before retirement.
*When to break the order:* no match means start at step 2. High-interest debt comes before all of it. If you're eligible for an HSA, read p4.

## p4 · Five accounts on one page
| account | who it's for | tax | catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 401(k) / 403(b) | through your employer | pre-tax or Roth | limited fund menu |
| Roth IRA | anyone under the income limit | tax-free growth | yearly limit |
| Traditional IRA | if Roth isn't available or a deduction helps | deductible (maybe) | taxed on withdrawal |
| HSA | high-deductible health plan | triple tax advantage | health plan required |
| Brokerage | everything else | taxed yearly on gains/dividends | no tax shelter, full flexibility |

## p5 · The three fund types
- **Total US stock index fund**: thousands of US companies in one fund.
- **Total international stock index fund**: the rest of the world.
- **Bond index fund**: steadier, lower expected growth, more of it as you near the money's use.
(Or one **target-date fund** that holds all three and adjusts over time.)
**Four numbers to check on any fund:** expense ratio (lower is better; broad index funds are often under 0.10%), what it holds, number of holdings (more is broader), and fund size.
This guide doesn't recommend specific funds. The mix between them depends on your timeline and comfort with risk.

## p6 · The one rule: automate before you optimise
Set an automatic transfer for the day after payday: bank → account → funds. Pick an amount you won't stop in a bad month. Raise it by 1% of pay each year. Then stop checking the app daily.

## p7 · A bad year
Markets fall, sometimes 20–30% or more, and recoveries can take years. The plan:
1. Money you need within roughly 3–5 years shouldn't be in stocks.
2. Keep the transfer running.
3. Don't sell because it's red.
4. Rebalance once a year, not every week.
None of this guarantees a result. It keeps you from the most common expensive mistake: selling low.
