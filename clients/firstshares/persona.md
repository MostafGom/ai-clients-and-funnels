# Persona — firstshares (First Shares)

**Who**: US salaried professionals aged 30–45: nurses, teachers, engineers, project managers, pharmacists, mid-level managers. Household income $70–150k. They have $20–80k in a savings account "for when I figure it out," and a 401(k) running on autopilot at whatever default fund HR picked. Many are married with kids, a mortgage or rent that eats a big slice of take-home, and a vague sense that friends who "got into stocks" in their twenties are now ahead. They read on their phone in the evening once the kids are down, mostly Reddit (r/personalfinance, r/Bogleheads), YouTube and the occasional money newsletter. They have a degree, they're good at their jobs, and they hate feeling stupid about something adults are "supposed to know."
**Awareness**: Problem-aware. They know the savings account is losing ground and that they "should be investing." They've heard "index funds" and "Roth IRA," but haven't chosen a path or moved a dollar. Many have opened a brokerage app once and closed it. They aren't yet sold on any particular solution, and they're deeply suspicious of anyone selling one.
**Pains**:
- "I've got $40k just sitting there and I *know* it's losing money every year. I feel sick about it."
- "What if I put it all in and the market crashes the next week? At least in savings it's safe."
- "ETF, index fund, expense ratio, Roth, traditional, brokerage… I open the app and there are 4,000 options and I just close it."
- "I've watched maybe 30 hours of YouTube on this and I'm more confused than when I started."
- "I tried a robo-advisor, didn't understand what it was doing, and stopped putting money in."
**Desires**:
- Their money set up properly, once: the right accounts opened, the right handful of funds chosen, contributions automated.
- To stop thinking about it. A plan they trust enough to leave alone through a bad year.
- To understand what they own and why, well enough to explain it to their partner.
- To stop feeling behind, and the quiet relief of "I'm finally doing the thing."
- To have a clear answer to "how much should I put in, and where, in what order?"
**Objections**:
- "This is all free on YouTube and Reddit. Why would I pay for it?"
- "I don't have time to watch the markets or learn to trade."
- "Is this another finance guru selling a dream? What's the catch, and what are you really selling me?"
- "What if I lose money?" (This one sits under all the others.)
**Language they use**: "sitting in savings," "losing to inflation," "I should be investing," "set it and forget it," "index funds," "Roth," "401(k) match," "I don't know where to start," "behind," "catch up," "too late," "boring," "automate," "just tell me what to do," "not trying to get rich, just not wanting to lose ground," "I froze."
**Words to avoid in copy**: "get rich," "financial freedom," "passive income machine," "beat the market," "guaranteed," "returns of X%," "secret," "hack," trading talk ("calls," "charts," "plays"). These read as the hype they're running from, and they break the compliance rules: First Shares is education, not investment advice.
**What they've tried**:
- YouTube and Reddit rabbit holes: hours of contradicting advice, no action.
- Opening a brokerage app: either froze at the fund screen or bought one random stock a coworker mentioned.
- A robo-advisor: started, didn't understand the allocation, stopped contributing after a red month.
**Proof that moves them**:
- **Process wins from peers**: people like them who *started*. "Opened my Roth at 41. Auto-investing $500 a month now. Took one Saturday." The proof is in accounts opened and contributions automated, never in returns.
- **Plain maths**: the cost of waiting, fee drag, what $500/month does over 20 years at an *illustrative* rate. Every number is labelled illustrative, not a forecast.
- **The founder's story**: Richard Hale (he/him), 54, spent 19 years on Wall Street as an equity analyst and then a fund-product manager `[CLIENT]`. He watched the industry sell complicated, expensive products to people who needed three cheap funds and an automatic transfer, and left in 2016 to teach them. Authority without hype: an insider who explains how it really works, takes no commissions and sells no funds.
- **A no-pitch promise**: First Shares never sells funds, never takes commissions and never manages money. Paired with a 30-day refund guarantee.

4. **Identity**: "For people who are good at their jobs and tired of feeling stupid about money."
