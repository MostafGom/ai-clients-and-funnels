## 3. The real problem (paper, with WakeWindowDial)
Heading: It isn't hunger. It isn't teeth. It's forty minutes.

Your baby isn't under-tired. She's *over*-tired. And overtired babies don't crash, they fight.

Here's the mechanism. Every baby has a wake window: the amount of awake time her body can handle before stress hormones kick in to keep her going. Miss the window by even forty minutes and cortisol floods in. Now she's wired. Bedtime takes an hour. And because cortisol hangs around, she pops awake at 2 a.m. ready to party.

That's it. That's the 2 a.m. party. Not a habit, not a phase, not something you caused. Timing.

Dial intro: Try it. Pick your baby's age and when her last nap ended. That's tonight's bedtime, and it's probably earlier than you think.

## 4. Why what you tried didn't work (paper)
Heading: You didn't fail. The advice did.

- **Letting her "cry it out."** Crying on top of cortisol is like turning the music up at a party. Some babies eventually crash from exhaustion, which is why it "works" for some and wrecks everyone else.
- **Rocking, feeding, bouncing to sleep.** Lovely, and it works, right up until she wakes between sleep cycles and needs the rocking again. At 2 a.m. That's not a bad habit, it's a missing skill, and it's teachable without tears.
- **A later bedtime so she's "more tired."** This is the one that gets almost everyone. Later bedtime, more cortisol, worse night. The fix is the opposite of what feels logical.
- **Trying a bit of everything.** Three days of one thing, two days of another. Babies learn from consistency. The method gives you one plan for seven nights, and that's the point.
