# The 7-Night Bedtime Reset
### The free plan for babies and toddlers (4 months to 3 years) who fight sleep. No cry-it-out.

## Page 1 · Read this first (2 minutes)
Your baby isn't a bad sleeper. She's probably forty minutes overtired.

That's the whole guide in one sentence, so if you only read one page, read this one.

Every baby has a **wake window**: the amount of awake time her body can handle before stress hormones switch on to keep her going. Miss the window and cortisol floods in. Now she's wired. Bedtime takes an hour. And because cortisol hangs around, she pops awake at 2 a.m. ready to party.

The fix is the opposite of what feels logical: **earlier, not later.** Get the timing right and most of the fighting disappears. Get it right for seven nights in a row and the 2 a.m. waking usually goes with it.

This Reset gives you three things: the chart that tells you tonight's bedtime (page 3), a ten-minute bedtime script (page 5), and seven nights, night by night (pages 6–9). There's a page for 2 a.m. (page 10), the five mistakes (page 11), and what to do on night eight (page 12).

**One rule for the week:** nobody cries it out. You stay with your baby. The plan changes timing and routine, nothing else. Feeding, medical care and safe-sleep guidance stay exactly as your pediatrician has them.

## Page 2 · How to use this guide
1. Print page 3 and stick it on the fridge.
2. Tonight, set bedtime from the chart, not from habit. It will feel early. Do it anyway.
3. Run the script on page 5. Same ten minutes, every night, in the same order.
4. Read tonight's page before bedtime, not during. Each night takes two minutes to read.
5. Keep a log. One line a night: bedtime, asleep time, each waking. You'll want it on night seven.

Who this is for: babies and toddlers from 4 months to 3 years. Under 4 months, newborn sleep is different; email hello@lumensleep.example.com.
Who should check with a doctor first: any baby with a medical condition affecting breathing, feeding or growth, and any baby whose pediatrician has flagged a sleep concern.

## Page 3 · The wake-window chart (print this)
Wake window = time from the end of the last nap to lights out.

| Age | Naps a day | Wake window before bed | If the last nap ended at 3:30 → lights out |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4–5 months | 3–4 | 1¾–2¼ h | 5:15–5:45 pm |
| 6–8 months | 2–3 | 2¼–3 h | 5:45–6:30 pm |
| 9–12 months | 2 | 3–3¾ h | 6:30–7:15 pm |
| 13–18 months | 1–2 | 3½–4½ h | 7:00–8:00 pm |
| 19 months – 3 years | 1 | 4½–6 h | 8:00–9:30 pm (aim for the early end) |

How to read it: find her age, add the window to the time her last nap ended, and aim for the **middle** of the range tonight. If she shows tired signs (rubbing eyes, staring, going quiet) before that, go earlier. If she gets giggly and wild, you've already missed it: go now.

Ranges are mainstream pediatric wake-window guidance for healthy babies; every baby sits somewhere inside her range. [CLIENT: Maya to sign off these values.]

## Page 4 · Why earlier works (the mechanism)
Think of cortisol as a second wind with a cost. When your baby stays awake past her window, her body releases it so she can keep going. It works. She looks fine, even energetic. That's the 7 p.m. giggles.

Then you try to put her down. Her body is still running on the second wind, so she fights. When she finally crashes, cortisol doesn't switch off. It fades slowly. Between sleep cycles, usually around 2 a.m., it's still high enough to wake her properly.

Move bedtime earlier, before the second wind, and three things happen over a few nights:
- bedtime gets shorter, because she's tired instead of wired;
- the first stretch gets longer, because she went down calm;
- the 2 a.m. waking fades, because there's less cortisol to do the waking.

Naps are the same mechanism in daylight. A 30-minute nap is usually a baby who went down overtired and couldn't link her sleep cycles.

## Page 5 · The 10-minute bedtime script
Same ten minutes, same order, every night. Her brain learns the sequence; by night three she'll start to settle at step two.

| Minute | Step | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Lights low, curtains closed, white noise on | "It's sleepy time." |
| 2–5 | Nappy, pyjamas, sleep sack. Slow hands, quiet voice. | Name what you're doing. "Arms in. Zip up." |
| 5–8 | One book or one song. The same one. | The same book or song every night. Boring is the point. |
| 8–9 | Cuddle, then into the cot awake or drowsy. | "I love you. Night night. I'm right here." |
| 9–10 | Hand on chest or back for a count of 20, then step back. | Nothing. Let the room be quiet. |

Three rules: no screens in the last hour; the last feed happens **before** the script, not as the last step; and once lights are out, you don't turn them back on.

## Page 6 · Night 1 · Dial the window
**Do:** set bedtime from the chart. Run the script. Into the cot awake or drowsy.
**Expect:** she may protest the early bedtime for 5–15 minutes. Stay in the room. Hand on chest, quiet voice, no lights. Many babies have their longest first stretch in months tonight.
**If she wakes:** go to page 10.
**Tonight's log:** bedtime ___ · asleep ___ · wakings ___

## Page 7 · Night 2 · Same again
**Do:** exactly what you did last night. Same bedtime (adjust only if the last nap moved), same script, same words.
**Expect:** less protest. If night one was rough, night two is usually easier, because her body is already catching up on sleep debt.
**Don't:** add anything new. Consistency is the lesson tonight.

## Page 8 · Night 3 · The 2 a.m. plan
**Do:** bedtime and script as before. Tonight, use page 10 for every waking, exactly as written.
**Expect:** this is where most families get the first 6-hour-plus stretch. It's also the night some babies test the new routine with a louder protest at the first waking. Stay. Do the plan. It's shorter than you fear.

## Page 9 · Nights 4–7 · Hold, then lock in
**Night 4 · Hold the line.** The night parents want to quit. Change nothing. If she's been sleeping better, a slightly worse night four is normal (it's often a nap or a tooth). Change nothing.
**Night 5 · Naps join the plan.** Use the chart for naps too: first nap at the short end of the window, lights low, mini-script (steps 1, 4 and 5 only).
**Night 6 · The long stretch.** For most babies, this is the night. Don't celebrate yet; just repeat it.
**Night 7 · Lock it in.** Look at your log. Compare night one and night seven. Then read page 12.

## Page 10 · The 2 a.m. decision tree (one page, no guessing)
She wakes. Before you move, ask in order:
1. **Has it been under 10 minutes since she fell asleep?** Wait 3 minutes. Most babies resettle between cycles.
2. **Is she crying, or grizzling?** Grizzling: wait 3 minutes, then go in. Crying: go in now.
3. **In the room:** lights off, voice low, hand on chest. "I'm here. It's sleepy time." Stay until she's calm. Pick her up only if she's distressed; put her down as soon as she's calm.
4. **Is it a feed window (your pediatrician's guidance for her age)?** Feed with lights off, no talking, back in the cot awake or drowsy.
5. **Is she standing?** Lay her down once, hand on back, same words. Don't make it a game.
6. **Repeat.** Same words, same calm, as many times as it takes. You never leave her to cry.

What this is not: it is not letting her cry it out. You are there for every minute.

## Page 11 · The five mistakes that cause early wakings
1. **Later bedtime to make her "more tired."** The most common one. More cortisol, worse night, earlier morning.
2. **Different routine on different nights.** Babies learn from repetition. Pick one script and keep it for seven nights.
3. **Feeding as the last step.** Feed before the script so sleep isn't tied to the bottle or breast.
4. **Lights on at 2 a.m.** Light tells her brain it's morning. Keep the room dark for every waking.
5. **Fixing nights but not naps.** Short, late naps push bedtime past the window. From night five, use the chart for naps.

## Page 12 · Night eight, and what comes next
If you ran seven nights, compare your log. Most families see a longer first stretch by night two, fewer wakings by night four, and the long stretch on night six or seven. If you're there, keep going exactly as you are for another week. It locks in.
